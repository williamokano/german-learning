# Exercise Patterns — C1

For grammar/vocabulary scope rules shared across all lesson reviews, see
`guidelines-C1.md`. **Always load both files** before reviewing.

This file defines what **exercise patterns** are appropriate at C1.

---

## Block structure

Same skeleton (34 exercises, ~180–220 items).

| Block | Exercises | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| H (Hören) | H1–H4 | Native-speed audio; complex topics |
| A (Basistraining) | A1–A12 | Complex syntax, idioms, register |
| B (Vertiefung) | B1–B9 | Academic / professional tasks |
| C (Prüfungstraining) | C1–C5 | telc C1 / Goethe C1 format |
| D (Wiederholung) | D1–D4 | Includes ≥20% from earlier levels |

**Note:** C1 lessons are planned but not yet authored in this project.
This guideline defines what C1 exercises **must** test and what they
**must avoid** (i.e. what would be C2).

---

## What's new at C1 vs B2

- **Complex sentence structure** — extended nominal phrases, deep embedding
- **Advanced connectors** — `zumal`, `sofern`, `insofern`, `als dass`
- **Idiomatic German** — Redewendungen, Sprichwörter, mode d'emploi
- **Nominalstil chains** — `das + nominalisierter Verb + multiple Genitiv-attribute`
- **Academic register** — research articles, abstracts
- **Subtle register shifts** — spoken vs written vs academic
- **Discourse cohesion** — text-level cohesion across paragraphs

---

## Exercise types at C1

### ✅ Use freely

All A1/A2/B1/B2 types plus:
- **Long complex order exercises** — 8–10 tiles with deep embedding
- **Gap-text with extended nominal phrases** — "Die sich aus der Nichtbeachtung ergebende Gefährdung" (use with care — over-nominalisation is a C1 trap)
- **Multi-gap text** with 15+ gaps testing complex syntax
- **Idiom-recognition exercises** — "Was bedeutet …?" with idiom identification
- **Register-shift exercises** — same content in formal vs informal
- **Complex single-choice** with subtle paraphrase distractors

### ⚠️ Use with care

- **Nominalisation chains** — 2–3 attributes is C1; more is C2
- **Verbs of perception + infinitive** — "Ich sehe ihn kommen" (without `zu`); "zu kommen" is non-standard
- **Modal particles** — placement changes meaning; test placement, not just recognition
- **Idioms** — context-critical; wrong context = wrong idiom

### ❌ Avoid at C1

- C2-only: extreme embedding, regional literary dialect, archaic forms
- Pure colloquial (4chan-style, meme-speak)
- Heavy bureaucratic/legal jargon

## Gap-text patterns

### Sentence length

- **Recommended:** 15–30 words
- **Maximum:** 35 words
- **Subordinate clauses:** up to 3 per sentence (deeply embedded)

### Nominalisation chains

- [ ] `das` + nominalised verb (neuter): `das Lesen`, `das Schreiben`
- [ ] `die` + `-ung` (feminine): `die Verarbeitung`, `die Umsetzung`
- [ ] `der` + `-er` (masculine agent): `der Leser`, `der Verarbeiter`
- [ ] Up to 3 Genitiv-attribute per chain; 4+ is C2
- [ ] Avoid over-nominalisation when a verbal clause is clearer (B2 students over-nominalise; flag if seen)

### Idiom exercises

- [ ] Idiom must be in context (don't test "Tomaten auf den Augen haben" in a chemistry text)
- [ ] Test meaning, not just recognition
- [ ] Include register note (informal vs formal idiom)

### Register-shift exercises

- [ ] Same content rendered in two registers (formal vs informal)
- [ ] Mark the register explicitly in the question
- [ ] Distractors include wrong-register versions

## gap-bank patterns

### H3

- Bank may include abstract C1 vocab, idioms, formal connectors
- Distractor plausibility: highly sophisticated (paraphrases, near-synonyms)

### C3

- 15 words, 10 gaps, 5 distractors
- Bank may include Konjunktiv I forms, complex nominalisation, idioms
- Cross-gap ambiguity check: especially important at C1 because forms are flexible

## Order-exercise patterns

### Tile count

- **C1 minimum:** 6 tiles
- **C1 maximum:** 10 tiles
- **Recommended:** 7–9 tiles

### Sentence structure

- All A1/A2/B1/B2 structures plus:
- **Complex nominal phrases** — "Die Verarbeitung der Daten ist wichtig" + extensions
- **Academic register** — research-article style
- **Idiomatic** — register-appropriate idiom placement
- **Modal particles** — `doch`, `mal`, `ja`, `halt`, `eben`, `schon` placement matters

### Verbs in tiles

- All conjugated
- Exception: All B2 exceptions + Konjunktiv I 3rd pl
- **NEW:** Verbs of perception: "Ich sehe ihn kommen" (no `zu`)

---

## Single-choice patterns

- Distractors may include C1 academic and idiomatic vocab
- H4 audio: native-speed (220+ wpm); complex topics (politics, science, society)
- Options may include subtle paraphrase distractors (formal vs informal, active vs passive)
- May include "no error" / "all correct" options (rare at A1/A2; common at C1)

---

## Categorize patterns

- 16–24 tokens
- 2–5 buckets
- **NEW:** Register categorisation (formal vs informal vs academic)
- **NEW:** Idiom vs literal categorisation
- **NEW:** Argumentative stance (pro/contra/neutral)

---

## Free-write patterns (C1 standard)

- **Length:** 10–15 sentences (essay-length)
- **Stimulus:** academic article, opinion piece, or research abstract
- **Use checklist:** 4+ bullet items including register and idiomatic language
- **Self-check:** 4+ form checks
- **Model answer:** present, demonstrates C1 register and idiomatic usage

---

## Common C1 exercise-schema errors to flag

1. **Over-nominalisation** — chains of 4+ attributes (C2 territory)
2. **Idiom out of context** — idiom doesn't fit the scenario
3. **Register mismatch** — academic vocab in spoken context, or vice versa
4. **Verbs of perception with `zu`** — "Ich sehe ihn zu kommen" (non-standard)
5. **Euphemism misapplication** — direct language where formal is needed, or vice versa
6. **False friends** — *aktuell* (current), *realisieren* (realise), *sensibel* (sensible)
7. **Heavy formal prepositions** — `hinsichtlich`, `bezüglich`, `infolge` (acceptable but heavy)

---

## CEFR-appropriate distractors

Distractors at C1 should:

- Be in C1 vocabulary range (subtle, idiomatic, academic)
- Include subtle register/idiom errors
- Be plausible paraphrase distractors
- NOT include C2-only literary archaisms or pure slang

## Example: acceptable vs unacceptable C1 exercises

| ❌ Unacceptable at C1 | Why | ✅ Acceptable alternative |
|------------------------|-----|--------------------------|
| Gap-text: "Die **sich aus der Nichtbeachtung der Sicherheitsvorschriften ergebende Gefährdung** der Mitarbeiter…" | Over-nominalisation (4+ attributes) | "Die Mitarbeiter werden gefährdet, **weil** die Sicherheitsvorschriften nicht beachtet werden." (verbal style) |
| Idiom: "Da **steppt der Bär** bei einer Beerdigung." | Wrong context for idiom | "Da steppt der Bär **bei der Party**!" (party context) |
| Single-choice: "**Hinsichtlich der Tatsache, dass**…" | Heavy prepositional chain (C2 register) | "**Da** …" or "**Weil** …" |
| False friend: "Das ist **aktuell** (= actually)" | Wrong meaning (aktuell = current) | "Das ist **tatsächlich**." (= actually) |
| Register mismatch: "**Ey, das ist ja voll krass!**" in academic context | Wrong register | "**Bemerkenswert.**" (academic register) |
