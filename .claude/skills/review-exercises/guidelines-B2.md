# CEFR Guidelines — B2 (Vantage)

**CEFR descriptor:** Can understand the main ideas of complex text on both
concrete and abstract topics, including technical discussions in his/her field
of specialisation. Can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that
makes regular interaction with native speakers quite possible without strain
for either party. Can produce clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects
and explain a viewpoint on a topical issue giving the advantages and
disadvantages of various options.

**Note for this course:** B2 lessons ship **in German only** (instruction
language switches from English to German at B2; this matches Telc/Goethe B2
exam conventions).

### Vocabulary

- Abstract nouns across domains (Wirtschaft, Wissenschaft, Gesellschaft)
- Nominalised verbs (das Lesen, die Verarbeitung)
- Phrasal verbs (in Frage stellen, zur Verfügung stellen)
- Compound nouns (4+ components: Bundesumweltamtsberichterstattung)
- Academic connectors (einerseits…andererseits, zumal, sofern)
- Politeness strategies in professional contexts
- Discourse markers for argumentation (zwar…aber, allerdings, immerhin)

### Can-do statements

- Read academic texts and summarise
- Write formal argumentative essays
- Participate in professional meetings
- Use passive voice fluently and choose the right form
- Use Konjunktiv I for reported speech
- Use Konjunktiv II for unreal conditions and wishes
- Distinguish nominal and verbal style (formal vs concise)
- Understand and produce charts and reports
- Discuss abstract topics (Wirtschaft, Wissenschaft, Gesellschaft)

### Grammar

- ❌ **Konjunktiv I Plusquamperfekt** (rare, even at C1)
- ❌ **Konjunktiv I future** (rare, even at C1)
- ❌ **Verbal style for academic register** (C1)
- ❌ **Nominal style chaining** (Nominalstil chains, *die-x-y-z* — more C1)
- ❌ **Complex academic register beyond B2 scope** (e.g. dissertation language)
- ❌ **Heavy anglicisms in formal writing** (only with established forms)
- ❌ **Subjunctive in counterfactual past + result clause** (rare, more C1)

### Vocabulary

- ❌ Heavy dialect vocabulary
- ❌ Slang beyond the colloquial standard
- ❌ Pure literary register

### Register

- ❌ Heavy informal/Internet register (4chan-style, meme-speak)

## Common learner errors at B2

1. **Zustandspassiv vs Vorgangspassiv** — students confuse
   - ❌ „Das Auto **ist** gekauft **worden**." (process, not state)
   - ✅ „Das Auto **ist** gekauft." (state, Zustandspassiv) vs „Das Auto **wird** gekauft." (process)
2. **`bekommen` + Passiv** — students misuse or over-use
   - ❌ „Ich **bekomme** geschenkt." (without Dativ recipient)
   - ✅ „Ich **bekomme** ein Buch **geschenkt**." (correct with direct object)
3. **Konjunktiv I forms** — students confuse 1st/3rd person
   - ❌ „Er sagt, er **habe** Zeit." (Konjunktiv I — wrong at B2 in indirect speech)
   - ✅ „Er sagt, er **habe** Zeit." (Konjunktiv I **ist correct** — students learn this is identical to Konjunktiv II in 1st/3rd sg, so use *er hätte* which is unambiguous)
4. **Funktionsverbgefüge** — students use the noun incorrectly
   - ❌ „Das **steht** zur **Verfügung**." (correct!) — *to make available* is „zur Verfügung stellen" (action) or „zur Verfügung stehen" (state)
5. **Partizip I/II as attribute position** — students use wrong forms
   - ❌ „Der **lesend** Mann." (Partizip I inflection wrong)
   - ✅ „Der **lesende** Mann."
6. **Nominalisation errors** — wrong article/gender for nominalised verbs
   - ❌ „Das **Essen** schmeckt gut." (correct) — *but*: „Die **Lesen** ist wichtig." (should be *das Lesen*, neuter)
7. **Wortbildung compounds** — students invent or mis-form compounds
   - ❌ „**Arbeitsuchung**" (should be *Arbeitsuche* or *Stellensuche*)
8. **Konjunktiv II past of modals** — `hätte + modal infinitive`
   - ❌ „Ich **hätte** gehen **sollen**." (correct!) — *but:* „Ich **würde** gehen **sollen**." (wrong; *würde* + *sollen* doesn't work)
9. **Reported speech tense confusion** — mixing Präteritum and Konjunktiv I
   - ❌ „Er sagte, er **hatte** Zeit." (incorrect at B2; should be Konjunktiv)
   - ✅ „Er sagte, er **habe** Zeit." (Konjunktiv I 3rd sg) or „er **hätte** Zeit" (Konjunktiv II fallback)
10. **Mixed nominal-verbal style** — students switch mid-sentence
    - ❌ „**Die Verarbeitung der Daten, was passiert, wenn** wir sie falsch einsetzen."
    - ✅ „**Die Verarbeitung der Daten** kann **problematisch sein**, wenn wir sie falsch einsetzen."

## Cross-language interference at B2

| L1              | Predictable B2 errors |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| English         | Zustandspassiv (English uses past participle as adjective, not as a separate form); Funktionsverbgefüge |
| Portuguese      | Konjunktiv I (no equivalent in Portuguese — most Romance speakers substitute infinitive); reported speech |
| Spanish         | Konjunktiv (Spanish subjunctive is required in more contexts than German); passive alternatives |
| French          | Subjonctif vs Konjunktiv (French uses it for emotion/uncertainty more broadly) |

## Example: acceptable vs unacceptable B2 sentences

| ❌ Unacceptable at B2 | Why | ✅ Acceptable alternative |
|------------------------|-----|--------------------------|
| „**Die sich mit dem Problem beschäftigend** Wissenschaftler…" | Participle as attribute is B2 but position must match inflection | „Die **sich mit dem Problem beschäftigenden** Wissenschaftler…" |
| „Das Auto **wurde** von mir gekauft **worden**." | Double passive | „Das Auto **wurde** von mir gekauft." |
| „Er sagt, er **habe** Zeit." | This **is** correct at B2 — but check 3rd person plural! | „Sie sagen, sie **hätten** Zeit." (Konjunktiv II fallback because 3rd pl sei/hätten is unambiguous) |
| „**Mangels** an Beweisen wurde er freigelassen." | `mangels` + Genitiv is too formal at B2 (C1) | „**Wegen fehlender** Beweise wurde er freigelassen." |
| „**Hinsichtlich** des Problems…" | Very formal preposition is C1+ | „**Bezüglich des** Problems…" or „**Was das** Problem **betrifft**, …" |
