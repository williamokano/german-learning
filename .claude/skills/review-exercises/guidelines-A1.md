# CEFR Guidelines — A1 (Breakthrough)

**CEFR descriptor:** Can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and
very basic phrases. Can introduce self and others, ask and answer questions
about personal details. Can interact in a simple way if the other person talks
slowly and clearly.

This file is the **authoritative scope rule** for any A1 lesson review. Every
reviewer MUST read it before applying §4 of `SKILL.md`.

---

## A1 Should-Have (in scope)

### Grammar (must be limited to these anchors)

| Lesson | Grammar anchor |
|--------|----------------|
| A1/01  | `sein`, regular present, personal pronouns, W-Fragen, statement & question word order |
| A1/02  | `haben`, possessive articles (mein/dein/sein/ihr), plural forms, yes/no questions, `nicht` |
| A1/03  | Definite/indefinite articles, negative article `kein`, accusative case (intro), `möchten` |
| A1/04  | Adjectives (predicative only), `es gibt` + Akkusativ, `gefallen` (intro) |
| A1/05  | Separable verbs, time expressions (um, am, von … bis) |
| A1/06  | Modal verb `können`, `gern/lieber/am liebsten`, irregular present (lesen, sehen, treffen, fahren) |
| A1/07  | Modals `müssen`, `wollen`, `dürfen`; Satzklammer |
| A1/08  | Prepositions with dative (mit, zu, von, bei, nach); `fahren` vs `gehen`; Sie-imperative |
| A1/09  | Dative personal pronouns, verbs with dative (gefallen, passen, gehören, helfen), `dieser`, `welcher?` |
| A1/10  | du/ihr-imperative, modal `sollen`, possessive articles in accusative/dative |
| A1/11  | Perfekt with `haben` and `sein`, participle formation (ge-…-t / ge-…-en), `war`/`hatte` |
| A1/12  | Ordinal numbers & dates, temporal prepositions (im, am, um, vor, nach) |
| A1/13  | Recap of cases, polite chunks (`könnten Sie…?`), filling in forms |
| A1/14  | Full review + mock exam |

### Vocabulary

- Numbers 0–1000
- Countries and languages (L1 = Russian, Brazilian Portuguese, Japanese, German, Austrian)
- Family members (Vater, Mutter, Bruder, Schwester, etc.)
- Food and drink (Kaffee, Tee, Brot, Brötchen, Apfel, …)
- Furniture and rooms (Wohnzimmer, Küche, Bad, Tisch, Stuhl, …)
- Hobbies (Fußball, Musik, Schwimmen, …)
- Professions (Lehrer, Arzt, Verkäuferin, …)
- Time expressions (Wochentage, Monate, Uhrzeiten)
- Greetings and farewells
- Modal particles: ja, doch, mal (sparingly, as chunks only)

### Can-do statements

- Greet and introduce yourself
- Spell the alphabet
- Ask and answer simple questions about family, work, hobbies
- Order food and drink in a café
- Ask for and understand prices
- Tell the time (official and colloquial)
- Describe your daily routine
- Talk about the weather and seasons
- Talk about a hobby using `gern / lieber / am liebsten`
- Describe your home (rooms, furniture, rent)
- Make an appointment
- Talk about a past event in Perfekt
- Fill in a simple form

## A1 Should-NOT-Have (out of scope — flag Critical)

### Grammar

- ❌ **Passive voice** (any form: werden + Partizip II, von + Dativ, sein + Partizip II as state passive)
- ❌ **Konjunktiv I** (reported speech; intro to subjunctive only at A2/09 with Konjunktiv II polite)
- ❌ **Konjunktiv II unreal** (only the polite `würde + Infinitiv` form is acceptable as a chunk)
- ❌ **Advanced adjective declension** (only predicative adjectives at A1; attributive = A2/08)
- ❌ **Genitive case** (except as fixed phrases like *des Herrn*, never as productive grammar)
- ❌ **Nominalised verbs** (no *das Essen*, *das Lesen* — those are B2)
- ❌ **Participles as adjectives** (Partizip I/II as attribute — B2)
- ❌ **Relative clauses** (Nom/Akk relatives are A2/13; genitive relatives are B1)
- ❌ **Subordinate clauses with verb-final** (only `weil`/`dass` as chunks; full treatment = A2/03)
- ❌ **Two-way prepositions with motion vs location** (introduced at A2/02; at A1 only Dativ forms)
- ❌ **Reflexive verbs in Dativ** (introduced at A2/05; at A1 only *sich fühlen* as a chunk)
- ❌ **Comparative and superlative declension** (only *gern → lieber → am liebsten* as a chunk)
- ❌ **Futur I** (introduced at A2/12; A1 uses Präsens for future meaning)
- ❌ **Präteritum narration** (only Präsens + Perfekt for narration; Präteritum = B1)
- ❌ **Infinitive clauses with `um…zu`, `ohne…zu`, `statt…zu`** (B1+)
- ❌ **Verbs with prepositions** (fixed-prep verbs = A2/06)
- ❌ **Adjective endings after definite/indefinite article** (Nom/Akk/Dat = A2/08)
- ❌ **`als` vs `wann`/`wenn`** (A1 uses *wann* for all time questions)

### Vocabulary

- ❌ Abstract nouns (Gesellschaft, Verantwortung, Entwicklung)
- ❌ Compound nouns of 4+ components
- ❌ Latinate/Greek roots beyond the basics (die Information at A1 is fine; die Implementierung is not)
- ❌ Anglicisms beyond chunks (no *downloaden* unless explicitly taught)
- ❌ Slang / youth language
- ❌ Fachsprache (academic, legal, medical, technical)
- ❌ Modal particles as productive grammar (only as chunks)
- ❌ False friends that would mislead (no *aktuell* meaning "current" until A2)

### Register

- ❌ Youth language (voll krass, geil, alter)
- ❌ Formal-academic register (in Anbetracht dessen, …)
- ❌ Dialect (Bairisch, Schwäbisch, Sächsisch)

### Other

- ❌ Long literary-style sentences (>20 words)
- ❌ Embedded subordinate clauses
- ❌ Implicit reference (no pronouns across long stretches)
- ❌ Cultural topics requiring real-world knowledge of Germany

## Common learner errors at A1

Predict and address these in exercises:

1. **Word order after `und`/`aber`** — students put the verb at position 3
   - ❌ „Ich wohne in Berlin und **bin** Student."
   - ✅ „Ich wohne in Berlin und **bin** Student." (V2 holds)
2. **Verb conjugation** — bare infinitive instead of conjugated form
   - ❌ „Ich **gehen** in die Schule." (English-speaker pattern)
   - ✅ „Ich **gehe** in die Schule."
3. **Capitalisation of nouns** — students lowercase common nouns
   - ❌ „Ich trinke **kaffee**."
   - ✅ „Ich trinke **Kaffee**."
4. **`sein` vs `haben` in Perfekt** — irregular aux selection
   - ❌ „Ich **habe** nach Hause gegangen." (L1 Portuguese influence)
   - ✅ „Ich **bin** nach Hause gegangen."
5. **`kein` vs `nicht`** — confusion about what each negates
   - ❌ „Ich habe **kein** Hunger." (= I have no hunger)
   - ✅ „Ich habe **keinen** Hunger." (den not ein!)
6. **Modal verb position** — conjugated modal in infinitive slot
   - ❌ „Ich **möchte** Deutsch **lerne**."
   - ✅ „Ich **möchte** Deutsch **lernen**." (Satzklammer)
7. **Personal pronoun gender** — `er` for `Yuki` (Japanese female name)
   - ❌ „Yuki kommt aus Japan. **Er** wohnt in Berlin."
   - ✅ „Yuki kommt aus Japan. **Sie** wohnt in Berlin."
8. **Separable verb prefix** — prefix glued to verb
   - ❌ „Ich **aufstehe** um 7 Uhr."
   - ✅ „Ich **stehe** um 7 Uhr **auf**."
9. **Dative vs accusative after verbs** — confusing *gefallen*/*passen* (Dativ) with *nehmen* (Akkusativ)
   - ❌ „Das Auto **gefallen mich**."
   - ✅ „Das Auto **gefällt mir**."
10. **`wann` vs `wo`** — confusing time and place questions
    - ❌ „**Wann** wohnst du?" (place question with *wo*)
    - ✅ „**Wo** wohnst du?"

## Cross-language interference at A1

Adjust error weighting by learner L1:

| L1              | Predictable A1 errors |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| English         | Word order (V2); articles (English has none); Perfekt (English uses Simple Past); false friends (bekommen, aktuell) |
| Portuguese      | Gender assignment differs (German masc = Port. fem and vice versa); verb-final subordinate clauses |
| Spanish         | Gender; articles (Spanish has them but case system differs); Perfect tense (Spanish uses it differently) |
| French          | Gender agreement (mostly agrees but masc/fem swaps); compound nouns |
| Italian         | Similar to Spanish + Port.; Perfekt is novel |
| Russian         | Cases align roughly but articles are missing in Russian; verb-final clauses |
| Japanese        | No grammatical gender at all; no articles; word order much freer |
| Arabic          | No grammatical gender in same way; SOV vs SVO difference |

The project's primary persona is **Bruno (Brazilian, Portuguese L1)** — weight
Portuguese errors most heavily in examples.

## Example: acceptable vs unacceptable A1 sentences

| ❌ Unacceptable at A1 | Why | ✅ Acceptable alternative |
|------------------------|-----|--------------------------|
| „Ich **hätte** gern einen Kaffee." | Konjunktiv II unreal is B1+ | „Ich **möchte** einen Kaffee." (möchten is taught at A1/03) |
| „Das Buch, **das** ich gelesen **habe**, war gut." | Relative clause with verb-final = A2/13+ | „Ich habe das Buch gelesen. Es war gut." |
| „**Wegen dem** Wetter bleibe ich zu Hause." | Genitive is B1+; use wegen + Dativ | „**Wegen dem** Wetter bleibe ich zu Hause." (acceptable at A2) — at A1 prefer: „Das Wetter ist schlecht. Ich bleibe zu Hause." |
| „Ich **gehe** zur Arbeit, **weil** ich **muss** Geld verdienen." | Verb position in *weil* clause is wrong | „Ich gehe zur Arbeit. Ich **muss** Geld verdienen." (keep *weil* as a chunk or use two sentences) |
| „**Obwohl** es regnet, gehe ich raus." | *obwohl* subordinate clause is B1/02 | „Es regnet, aber ich gehe raus." (use *aber* coordination) |
| „Das ist **das** Auto **meines** Bruders." | Genitive pronoun + article declension = B1+ | „Das ist **das** Auto **von meinem Bruder**." (von + Dativ = A2) |
| „Ich **würde** gern schwimmen gehen." | *würde* is Konjunktiv II polite = A2/09 | „Ich **möchte** schwimmen gehen." or chunk: „Ich **würde** gern…" with teacher note |
| „**Wenn** ich Zeit hätte, würde ich kommen." | Konjunktiv II real conditional = B1 | „**Wenn** ich Zeit **habe**, komme ich." (present-tense conditional acceptable) |
